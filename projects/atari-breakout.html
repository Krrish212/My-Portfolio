Does algorithmic sophistication pay off under a fixed interaction budget?

Breakout is a hard control problem for a specific reason: the agent sees only raw pixels, rewards arrive only on brick hits, and the behaviours that matter — positioning, aiming, sustaining a rally — are reinforced indirectly and long after the decisions that caused them. That combination of high-dimensional input, sparse reward, and long-horizon credit assignment is what separates the algorithms.

We implemented four agents against the same environment and the same Nature CNN trunk, so the only thing that varies is the learning algorithm. A vanilla DQN baseline, a Double Dueling DQN with two targeted fixes, Rainbow with six extensions stacked together, and PPO as an on-policy contrast. Each trained for 5 million steps across three seeds on a single L40S.

The ordering was not the expected one. Double Dueling DQN won, vanilla DQN came a close second, PPO trailed, and Rainbow — the most complete agent, and the most expensive to run — collapsed to roughly a fifth of the baseline's score and stayed there. The interesting part of this project is the diagnosis of why.

04Results

Mean evaluation reward over training

Rolling mean across 3 seeds · evaluated every 100k steps, 20 episodes per eval
Double Dueling DQN DQN PPO Rainbow DQN

Double Dueling DQN separates from the pack early and never gives the lead back, finishing at 224.91 with the tightest seed spread of any agent. Vanilla DQN tracks it closely the whole way, which is itself a result: on this domain, under this budget, the two targeted fixes buy about 7% over a plain baseline and everything beyond that buys nothing.

PPO climbs more slowly and finishes at 164.15 with a seed standard deviation of ±29.03, four times the spread of the DQN variants. That is the on-policy trade-off made visible: PPO reuses each rollout for four epochs and then discards it, while the replay-based agents can resample a rare brick-hit transition hundreds of times. Under a fixed step budget, that reuse matters.

All four sit well below published numbers, and the reason is budget rather than method. 5M steps is 20M frames — 10% of the 200M frames these benchmarks are normally run for. Every curve except Rainbow's is still rising at the cutoff.

05Why Rainbow failed

Six extensions, 25% more compute, and a fifth of the score. Rainbow's value estimates stopped improving at one million steps and never recovered.

The failure was diagnosed from telemetry rather than guessed at. Rainbow's q_val and q_mean both rise sharply through the first million steps, then flatten and stay flat for the remaining four — and the reward curve flattens at exactly the same point. Value estimates that stop moving while training continues indicate convergence to a low-return local optimum, not a crash or an implementation bug.

The likely cause is interaction between components. Rainbow stacks six mechanisms, each carrying its own hyperparameters: PER's α and β schedule, the 3-step return horizon, NoisyNet σ, and C51's atom count and support bounds. We had budget to tune learning rate and nothing else. Poorly calibrated distributional support or over-aggressive prioritisation will quietly degrade the target signal without producing an obvious error.

The practical conclusion is that complexity is a liability under a fixed tuning budget. Rainbow was dominated on both axes — worst score and highest wall clock — while Double Dueling DQN got the best result for essentially no extra compute. The follow-up is a controlled ablation adding one component at a time to the Double Dueling baseline.

06How the codebase is built

Experiments are defined by config objects, not by scripts.

Four algorithms across three seeds is twelve training runs, plus a learning-rate sweep on top. Keeping those reproducible mattered as much as the algorithms themselves.

Registry with inferred configs

A @register_agent("ppo") decorator maps a name to its class, and reads the config type straight off the generic base — BaseAgent[PPOConfig] — so the pairing can't drift out of sync.

CLI generated from dataclasses

Every hyperparameter is a dataclass field, and the argument parser is built by reflecting over those fields. Adding a hyperparameter gives you a CLI flag for free, with no parser edits.

Run config saved with the weights

The full configuration — env settings, seed, agent hyperparameters — is written as JSON beside each checkpoint. test and record reload it, so evaluation can never silently diverge from training.

Honest evaluation path

Reward clipping and episodic-life termination are training-only. Evaluation rebuilds the environment without them and runs the real game, every 100k steps across 20 episodes.
